Client Portal

Your portal,
your clients.

The client portal gives your buyers and sellers their own private window into their deal — no login required, no app to install. This guide walks you through everything, from sending the first link to handling a contract crash.

Panda Realty · May 2026
01

Generating Portal Links

Every client gets their own unique, private link — buyers and sellers each have a separate one, and neither can see the other's view. You generate these from the admin panel and send them however you like: email, SMS, WhatsApp. No login required on the client's end.

For the seller

The seller portal panel lives in the Owners section at the top of the property's edit dialog — it's always visible without clicking into any tab.

1Open the property's edit dialog. Scroll up if needed — the Seller Portal panel is in the Owners section, above the tabs.
2Click Generate seller link — the link appears immediately.
3Click Copy and paste it into an email or message to your seller.
4The panel shows the date and time the seller last opened the link, so you can confirm they've actually viewed it.

Seller portal access panel — generate, copy, last-accessed timestamp, and revoke

For the buyer

The buyer's portal link lives inside their profile dialog, under the Portal tab.

1Open the property's edit dialog and go to the Buyers tab.
2Click Open Profile on the relevant buyer — their workspace dialog opens.
3Click the Portal tab inside the buyer's workspace.
4Click Generate buyer link, copy it, and send it to your buyer.

Buyer portal access panel — URL, Copy, last-accessed timestamp, and Revoke

Links don't expire on their own. They stay active until you revoke them. If your seller accidentally shares their link, or you want to reset access for any reason, hit Revoke and generate a fresh one — the old link goes dead immediately and the new one works straight away.

02

Sharing Documents

Files you upload don't automatically appear in the portal — you decide exactly what each client sees. Every file has two independent toggles: one for the buyer, one for the seller. You can share a contract draft with your buyer only, a solicitor's letter with your seller only, or flip both on to share the same file with everyone.

1Open the property's edit dialog and go to the Files tab.
2Find the file you want to share. Each row shows two switches on the right — buyer and seller.
3Toggle Share with buyer, Share with seller, or both.
4The file appears in the relevant portal straight away — no refresh needed on their end.
5To hide a file again, toggle the switch off. It disappears from the portal immediately.

Files tab — two independent share toggles per file (buyer left, seller right)

If a client says they can't see a file, check this panel first. Files are hidden by default even after you upload them — you have to actively switch them on. This is intentional: upload when you have the file, share when you're ready for the client to see it.

03

The Buyer's View

Here's what your buyer sees the moment they open their link — a clean, no-login portal with everything relevant to their purchase. No account to create, no app to install. The link works on any device.

Property summary

The first thing they land on: the property address, type, bedrooms/bathrooms, and the contract and settlement dates. Once an offer is accepted, the dates populate automatically from the offer details.

Buyer portal — property summary with address, spec, and key dates

Conditions timeline

All the contract conditions are listed here with their due dates. A green tick means a condition has been cleared; a clock icon means it's still pending. Your buyer can see at a glance which conditions are still open and exactly how many days are left on each one.

Buyer portal — conditions timeline with status indicators and due dates

Documents

Only the files you've toggled on in the admin Files tab appear here. Your buyer can download any shared file but can't upload their own. If this section is empty, head back to the Files tab and check the share toggles.

Buyer portal — documents tab showing shared files

Messages

Your buyer types their message here and hits send — you'll get an email notification and can reply from the admin panel. The thread shows the full conversation history. Messages are capped at 2,000 characters, which is plenty for a detailed update.

Buyer portal — messages tab with compose area

04

The Seller's View

The seller's portal shares the same clean design as the buyer's but surfaces additional information only relevant to the seller — most importantly, the sale price and the full offer history. Their message thread is also completely isolated from the buyer's.

Summary — with sale price

The sale price row sits prominently at the top of the summary card. Buyers never see this — it only appears in the seller's view. Contract date, settlement date, and property details are shared across both portals.

Seller portal — summary showing sale price (buyers never see this row)

Conditions

The conditions tab mirrors what the buyer sees — same dates, same status indicators. This gives your seller visibility into how the deal is progressing without you needing to call them with every update.

Offers history

Every offer submitted on the property appears here — amount, submission date, and whether it was accepted, rejected, or lapsed. The currently accepted offer is clearly marked. This gives your seller a complete picture of how the sale came together.

Seller portal — full offer history with accepted offer indicator

Messages

The seller's message thread is completely private — they cannot see buyer messages, and you can't accidentally send a reply into the wrong thread. Each client only ever sees their own conversation with you.

Seller portal — private seller message thread (isolated from buyer)

05

Messaging

When a client sends you a message through their portal, you get an email notification. You reply directly from the admin panel — no need to log into the portal or switch between systems. Buyer and seller messages live in separate tabs inside the one panel, so you always know whose thread you're reading.

1Open the property's edit dialog and go to the Buyers tab.
2Click Open Profile on the relevant buyer — their workspace dialog opens.
3Inside the buyer's workspace, click the Messages tab.
4You'll see two sub-tabs: Buyer and Seller. A blue dot on a tab means there's an unread message waiting.
5Click the relevant tab, type your reply in the text box, and hit Send.

Admin messages panel — Buyer and Seller tabs, unread dot indicator, reply box

Both threads in one place. You don't need to open a separate dialog to reply to your seller — just switch tabs within the same Messages panel. The unread dot clears as soon as you open the thread.

06

When a Deal Falls Through

When a contract crashes and a new buyer comes in, the Contract Crash button handles the full reset in one go. You don't need to manually revoke links, reset document sharing, or juggle tokens — it all happens atomically the moment you confirm.

What Contract Crash does

Six things happen simultaneously when you confirm:

1Un-accepts the current offer — the deal pipeline stage resets to active.
2Revokes the buyer's portal link — the old buyer's link goes dead immediately. If they try to open it, they'll see an access-revoked page.
3Clears all buyer document shares — every file that was shared with the buyer is hidden. File toggles are reset to off.
4Revokes the seller's existing portal link — the old link is killed.
5Generates a new seller portal link — your seller gets a fresh link ready to send, reflecting the reset deal.

Contract Crash confirmation dialog — all six actions listed before you confirm

How to trigger it

1Open the property's edit dialog. The Contract Crash button is in the Owners section — the same place as the seller portal panel.
2Click Contract Crash. Read through the confirmation dialog — it lists every action that will happen.
3Click Confirm Contract Crash.
4Copy the seller's new portal link from the Owners section and send it to them.
5Once you accept a new offer, the conditions timeline will update automatically for the next buyer.

This can't be undone. Once you confirm, all six changes happen together — there's no partial rollback. Contract Crash only works when the property has a currently accepted offer; if you see an error, check that the offer is marked as accepted first.
